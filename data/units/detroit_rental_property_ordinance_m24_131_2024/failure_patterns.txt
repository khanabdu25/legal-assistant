FAILURE PATTERNS — DETROIT 2024 RENTAL PROPERTY ORDINANCE

Purpose

This document records source-specific retrieval and answer-grounding
failure patterns to test for when working with the Detroit 2024 Rental
Property Ordinance (M24-131, 2024).

Use this as a living test guide: 1. Turn each pattern into one or more
retrieval/answer tests. 2. Inspect the actual source units returned by
retrieval. 3. Record failures that are observed during testing. 4. Fix
the appropriate layer (source representation, chunking, retrieval,
cross-reference handling, or answer generation). 5. Update this document
when normalization reveals a new structural risk or testing exposes a
new failure.

Status labels

[STRUCTURAL RISK] A potential failure inferred from the source’s
structure. [OBSERVED FAILURE] A failure actually reproduced during
testing. [RESOLVED] A previously observed failure that has been fixed
and retested.

Do not label a pattern as observed merely because it is plausible.

Scope and current coverage

The initial version is based on the ordinance material normalized so
far: - Definitions sections: §§ 8-15-3 through 8-15-9. - Registration of
rental property: §§ 8-15-81 and 8-15-82.

Other provisions of the 2024 amendment may introduce additional
patterns. Add them as those provisions are processed. Do not assume this
list exhausts the ordinance’s failure modes.

A. DEFINITION RETRIEVAL

Definition is not retrieved because the question uses a synonym or
paraphrase rather than the ordinance’s defined term. [STRUCTURAL
RISK] Test: Ask about a concept using ordinary language, then check
whether the corresponding defined term and its complete definition
are retrieved. Failure looks like: a related provision is returned,
but the definition that establishes the ordinance’s precise meaning
is absent.

Only part of a definition is retrieved. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask
about definitions containing multiple numbered conditions,
subparagraphs, thresholds, or qualifying phrases. Failure looks
like: the answer includes the opening sentence but omits one or more
conditions, exceptions, measurements, or subparagraphs.

A definition is confused with a similarly named or related
definition. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask separate questions about
closely related terms (for example, lead-based paint, lead-based
paint hazard, lead inspection, lead clearance, dust-lead violation,
and risk assessment). Failure looks like: the answer combines
requirements from different definitions or attributes one term’s
criteria to another.

A definition is retrieved without enough context to identify its
exact defined term or section. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Retrieve
individual definition child units and verify that the returned unit
retains its term, parent section, and source citation. Failure looks
like: the text is returned but its defined-term label or statutory
location is missing or ambiguous.

The model treats a definition as a general legal definition rather
than the ordinance’s own definition. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask
questions where ordinary usage may differ from the ordinance’s
defined meaning. Failure looks like: the answer substitutes common
meaning, another law’s definition, or outside knowledge for the
ordinance’s actual wording.

B. NUMBERED CONDITIONS, THRESHOLDS, AND QUALIFIERS

One or more numbered or lettered elements are omitted. [STRUCTURAL
RISK] Test: Ask for all elements of definitions or provisions
containing numbered lists, such as multi-part definitions and §
8-15-81(a)’s application requirements. Failure looks like: the
answer gives only some list items while presenting the list as
complete.

A numeric threshold, unit, comparison, or qualifying condition is
lost or altered. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask for exact values and
conditions in definitions, including percentages, resident counts,
measurements, time periods, and other numeric criteria. Failure
looks like: a number is rounded, changed, detached from its unit, or
reported without the condition that makes it applicable.

A qualifier changes the meaning but is dropped from the answer.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask about provisions containing phrases such
as “at least,” “only if,” “unless,” “as defined,” or other limiting
language. Failure looks like: the answer states a broad rule where
the source states a narrower or conditional one.

Separate subparagraphs are collapsed into one generalized rule.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask for the individual criteria in
definitions with numbered subparagraphs and nested lettered items.
Failure looks like: the answer summarizes the general topic but does
not preserve which criterion belongs to which subparagraph.

Conditional procedures or exceptions are retrieved without their
controlling conditions. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask questions where
the applicable procedure depends on a condition, classification,
location, or exception, and verify that the answer preserves both
the rule and the condition determining when it applies. Failure
looks like: one branch is presented as universal, or an exception
or prerequisite is omitted.

C. STATUTORY SECTION AND SUBSECTION RETRIEVAL

A question about one subsection retrieves only the parent section or
a neighboring subsection. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask narrowly about
each subsection of § 8-15-81(a)–(g). Failure looks like: the section
heading or general section text is returned, but the specific
subsection containing the answer is absent.

The parent section is not included when child-subsection context is
necessary. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Retrieve a subsection and check
whether the system retains its section number, section title, and
parent-child relationship. Failure looks like: the text is retrieved
without enough context to identify that it belongs to § 8-15-81 or
what subject the section covers.

A child subsection is retrieved without its internal list or
complete operative text. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask for all
application requirements under § 8-15-81(a), and for the complete
obligations under each of (b)–(g). Failure looks like: only the
first sentence or a fragment of the subsection is available to the
answer generator.

The model merges distinct subsection obligations. [STRUCTURAL RISK]
Test: Ask separately about false information, registration validity,
registry maintenance, contact-information updates, ownership
transfers, and annual fees under § 8-15-81. Failure looks like: an
obligation is attributed to the wrong subsection or the conditions
for one obligation are applied to another.

D. CROSS-REFERENCES AND RELATED PROVISIONS

A provision expressly referenced by the retrieved text is not
retrieved. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask about an application
requirement in § 8-15-81(a)(4), which refers to § 8-15-84, and
inspect whether the referenced provision is needed and available.
Failure looks like: the answer quotes the referring subsection but
omits the referenced provision needed to explain the requirement
completely.

A cross-reference is mistaken for the substantive rule itself.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask what a subsection requires when it
points the reader to another section. Failure looks like: the answer
repeats the section number but does not retrieve or explain the
referenced provision.

A related provision is retrieved merely because it is semantically
similar, even though it is not the provision expressly referenced.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Compare the exact statutory cross-reference
with other related provisions returned by semantic search. Failure
looks like: the answer relies on a neighboring or topical provision
instead of following the actual reference.

Cross-reference context is lost when definition child units are
retrieved independently. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask about a defined
term whose definition points to, incorporates, or depends on another
section, regulation, or external legal instrument. Failure looks
like: the definition is quoted without the incorporated material
needed to understand its scope.

Note: Explicit cross-reference edges are a candidate improvement, not an
assumption that every reference must always be followed. Testing should
establish when following a reference is necessary for a complete answer.

E. EXACT QUOTATION AND SOURCE FIDELITY

The answer paraphrases when the question asks for the exact source
text. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask for the exact wording of a
definition or subsection. Failure looks like: the response conveys
the general meaning but changes wording, punctuation, qualifiers, or
sentence structure.

The answer silently corrects unusual wording, grammar, punctuation,
or apparent source errors. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask for exact
quotations of passages that were flagged during normalization as
unusual or potentially anomalous. Failure looks like: the answer
produces a polished or corrected version instead of the wording
present in the source.

Text from separate definitions or provisions is combined into a
quotation that does not exist in the source. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test:
Ask for a quotation supporting a multi-part answer and compare it
character-by-character with the source units. Failure looks like:
the answer presents stitched-together text as though it were one
continuous statutory quotation.

The answer omits a sentence or clause while presenting a quotation
as complete. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask for the complete definition
or complete subsection, especially where it contains multiple
sentences or nested elements. Failure looks like: the quotation
starts and ends plausibly but skips substantive text in the middle
or at the end.

The answer fails to distinguish source wording from explanation.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask both what the ordinance says and what
that means in plain language. Failure looks like: explanatory
paraphrase is placed inside quotation marks or is attributed to the
ordinance.

F. SOURCE LOCATION, CITATION, AND HIERARCHY

The answer cites the wrong section, subsection, or defined term.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: For each retrieved answer, verify the source
location against the canonical JSON and original ordinance. Failure
looks like: the quoted text is relevant but the citation points to a
neighboring provision or the wrong definition.

The answer gives a parent-section citation when a more precise
subsection citation is available. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Ask
questions answered by a particular subsection of § 8-15-81. Failure
looks like: the answer cites § 8-15-81 generally without identifying
the relevant subsection.

Definition hierarchy is lost. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Retrieve
definitions from each definitions section and verify that the term
remains associated with its correct parent section and definition
index. Failure looks like: a definition is assigned to the wrong
section, duplicate terms become indistinguishable, or
ordering/context is lost.

G. NORMALIZATION AND DATA-INTEGRITY RISKS

A source-text anomaly is silently normalized away. [STRUCTURAL RISK]
Test: Compare canonical full_text against the verified source for
passages flagged during extraction. Failure looks like: an unusual
phrase, apparent typo, repeated phrase, malformed citation, or other
source feature disappears without an explicit user-approved
correction.

A child unit is missing, duplicated, or associated with the wrong
parent. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Validate child-unit counts, IDs,
parent IDs, definition indices, and parent derived_child_unit_ids
arrays. Failure looks like: a definition cannot be retrieved because
its unit was never created, or it appears under the wrong
definitions section.

Structured content and full_text disagree. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test:
Compare every structured definition/subsection with its
corresponding full_text. Failure looks like: a structured field
contains a shortened or altered version, or the full text contains
content not represented in the structured fields.

A JSON or metadata error causes content to be dropped or misread.
[STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: Parse every JSON file; check for duplicate
keys, invalid syntax, missing required fields, and inconsistent
metadata. Failure looks like: a parser rejects the file, silently
overwrites a duplicate key, or ingestion omits a unit or field.

A source correction is applied inconsistently across parent and
child units. [STRUCTURAL RISK] Test: For every user-approved
correction, compare the parent text, child text, structured
representation, and any derived retrieval text. Failure looks like:
one representation contains the approved correction while another
retains the superseded text.

H. HOW TO USE THESE PATTERNS DURING TESTING

For each test, record: - Test question. - Expected source
section/subsection/definition. - Expected supporting text or required
elements. - Actual retrieved unit IDs and source locations. - Whether
the retrieved material was sufficient. - Whether the generated answer
quoted or summarized it accurately. - Failure category and observed
behavior. - Fix applied and retest result.

Diagnose failures in this order: 1. SOURCE / NORMALIZATION: Is the
needed text present and correct in the canonical source data? 2.
CHUNKING / UNIT DESIGN: Does the needed text exist in a retrieval unit
that can be retrieved without losing necessary context? 3. RETRIEVAL:
Did the correct unit(s) reach the answer generator? 4. CONTEXT ASSEMBLY:
Were necessary parent units, numbered elements, or referenced provisions
included? 5. GENERATION / GROUNDING: Given sufficient source text, did
the model quote and explain it accurately? 6. CITATION: Does the answer
point to the exact source location?

Do not treat every incomplete answer as a retrieval failure. If the
necessary source material was not retrieved, investigate retrieval and
context assembly. If it was retrieved but the answer omitted or altered
it, investigate generation and grounding.
